    About Kapalabhati & safety

    Kapalabhati ("skull-shining breath") is a cleansing practice from hatha yoga. The exhale is short and active — a sharp snap of the lower belly pushes air out through the nose — and the inhale happens on its own as the belly relaxes. It's energising and warming, and traditionally followed by a breath hold (kumbhaka) and quiet, slower breathing to settle the nervous system.

    Technique: sit tall, mouth closed, breathe through the nose. Keep the chest and shoulders still; only the belly moves. Start slow — clean, even strokes matter more than speed.

    Skip or go gentle if you are pregnant or menstruating heavily, have high blood pressure, heart disease, epilepsy, a hernia, glaucoma, or recent abdominal surgery. Practise on an empty stomach. Never practise breath holds in or near water, or while driving. If you feel dizzy or tingly, stop and breathe normally — lightheadedness means slow down.
